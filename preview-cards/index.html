What problem does Rise solve?

The Australian government now requires 245,000 NDIS providers to be registered to operate. There are only 20 auditing bodies, and each audit takes 30 hours, creating an 840-year backlog to a compliant sector. Large compliance platforms exist but niche markets are completely underserved and looking for solutions.

How does Rise solve the problem?

Under the NDIS, 245,000 providers must register through just 20 auditors, each taking 30 hours per audit, an 840-year backlog. Rise automates compliance through a shared digital workspace that connects providers and auditors, cutting audit time from 30 to 10 hours and saving providers up to $280K a year, while improving outcomes for people in care.

Before you call.

What counts as a participant?

Anyone you are actively supporting under your registration. People you have exited are not counted, so the number follows your real caseload rather than your history.

What happens if I grow past my band mid-year?

Nothing breaks and nothing stops. Your size is reviewed at renewal rather than the moment you cross a threshold, so taking on a new participant never triggers an invoice.

Do I pay per worker?

No. Workers, logins, screening records, training records and incident logs are unlimited on every plan. Charging per user would bill you for putting evidence in, which is exactly what an auditor asks to see.

Why does the audit pathway change the price?

Certification carries a second auditor, a two-stage audit and an 18-month mid-term audit, so there is materially more to prepare and keep current. Verification is a single desktop audit. Your pathway is printed on your certificate of registration.

Is the figure on this page a quote?

No, it is indicative. It is built from your size and your pathway so you can get close before speaking to anyone, and it is confirmed on the call.

What if I am not registered yet?

That is the most common place to start. Rise covers class selection, the evidence you need and the audit itself, so a first registration is the thing it was built for.
